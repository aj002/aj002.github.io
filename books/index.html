books

A slowly expanding shelf of books I keep thinking about, stealing frameworks from, or recommending too aggressively.

This is less a canon and more a map of recurring obsessions: competence, weird systems, friendship under pressure, invented worlds, and mathy futures. Titles link to Goodreads. Start with the all-time shelf; everything below it is sorted by the itch it scratches.

The All-Time Shelf

The books I actually push into people's hands. Longer notes because I can't help it.

epic fantasycomfort rereadall-time
The Lord of the Rings
J.R.R. Tolkien

The book I measure other worlds against. I love that it refuses to be efficient — the walking, the songs, the genealogies all make Middle-earth feel older than the story passing through it. Every reread I find myself attached to a different character, which I think is the real test of a book: it changes because I did. When everything in my life is optimized for speed, this is my reminder that endurance is also a skill.

systems epicall-time
Dune
Frank Herbert

I came for the sandworms and stayed for the incentive design. Ecology, religion, spice markets, imperial politics — it reads like someone stress-testing a civilization and publishing the results as a novel. Spending my days around markets has only made it better: the idea that every lever you pull has a second-order consequence waiting feels less like fiction and more like documentation.

space puzzleall-time
Project Hail Mary
Andy Weir

I expected to enjoy the engineering puzzles, and I did. I did not expect the friendship to be the thing that stuck — it turns a competence fantasy into something much warmer. It is the most sincere book I have read in years, and I finished it in two sittings and immediately started recommending it to people who claim they don't read sci-fi.

short storiesrewired me
Exhalation
Ted Chiang

Ted Chiang writes the way I wish proofs read: one clean idea, carried carefully, with a human cost attached. I ration these stories because reading two in a row feels greedy. If you only read one, make it the title story — it is the gentlest piece of existential dread I know.

Myth, Wonder, Old Magic

Books I read for atmosphere as much as plot. I like when a world feels lived in before the first page starts.

science fantasychildhood read
A Wrinkle in Time
Madeleine L'Engle

Written for kids who are too intense for their age and know it. The tesseract math is really emotional geometry, but Meg's anger and loyalty still feel precise.

quiet labyrinthloved
Piranesi
Susanna Clarke

A tiny book that feels enormous. It treats solitude as something sacred without pretending it is harmless.

Competence, Contact, Survival

The part of the shelf for smart people doing constrained problem-solving under absurd pressure.

survival mathloved
The Martian
Andy Weir

Define the problem, inventory the constraints, do the next useful thing. Optimism as an operating procedure, not a mood.

first contact
Contact
Carl Sagan

Really a book about evidence and longing, and how hard it is to stay intellectually honest when the stakes turn personal.

big dumb object
Rendezvous with Rama
Arthur C. Clarke

The restraint is the charm — it never over-explains the mystery. Awe, left deliberately unresolved.

evolutionary epicrewired me
Children of Time
Adrian Tchaikovsky

The best "intelligence doesn't have to look like us" book I know. I came out of it with genuine respect for spiders, which I resent.

cosmic dreadstill arguing with it
The Three-Body Problem
Cixin Liu

Cold, strange, not cuddly. Civilization as a fragile local minimum rather than a guarantee.

Posthuman Brainworms

Messier books, sharper edges. I like them because they feel like futures with too many tabs open.

singularity fictionrewired me
Accelerando
Charles Stross

Not cozy reading; a bandwidth event. Technology outrunning manners, markets, and families in real time.

cyberpunk
Snow Crash
Neal Stephenson

Maximalist and ridiculous in exactly the way early internet mythology deserves. Velocity forgives everything, including a hero named Hiro Protagonist.

classic cyberpunk
Neuromancer
William Gibson

Mostly vibes, but historically important vibes — the aesthetic operating system half of tech culture still runs on.

hostile cognitionstill arguing with it
Blindsight
Peter Watts

Unpleasant in a productive way. Consciousness as a weird, expensive side effect instead of a crown.

identity mathrewired me
Permutation City
Greg Egan

Fiction behaving like a philosophical proof with vertigo. Takes copies and simulated selves more seriously than is comfortable.

Systems, Risk, Taste

Books I want near the same shelf as markets, research, taste, and how people fool themselves.

probabilitystill arguing with it
Fooled by Randomness
Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Annoying and useful, which is a powerful combination. A warning label for narratives that are too smooth after the fact.

risk history
Against the Gods
Peter L. Bernstein

Humans slowly learning to put numbers on uncertainty, then immediately getting overconfident about the numbers.

recursioncomfort reread
Gödel, Escher, Bach
Douglas Hofstadter

A brilliant attic of recursion, minds, music, and self-reference. I wander in for one chapter and lose an afternoon.

progressstill arguing with it
The Beginning of Infinity
David Deutsch

I argue with half of it, which is exactly why I keep it around. Takes explanations more seriously than almost anything else I've read.
